I.       INTRODUCTION

         A.     Overview

        This proceeding began on April 27, 2023, when the Federal Maritime Commission
(“Commission” or “FMC”) issued a notice of filing of complaint and assignment, indicating that
Bed Bath & Beyond Inc. (“BBBY” or “BBB”), currently known as 20230930-DK-Butterfly-1, 2
had filed a complaint against Respondents Orient Overseas Container Line Limited and OOCL
(Europe) Limited (collectively “OOCL”).

         BBBY’s amended complaint alleges five violations:
     •   Count I: 46 U.S.C. § 41102(c), unreasonable practices for failing to meet service
         commitments (space allocation and premium pricing overcharges);
     •   Count II: 46 U.S.C. § 41102(c) and 46 C.F.R. § 545.5, unreasonable practices for
         detention and demurrage;
     •   Count III: 46 U.S.C. § 41104(a)(2), service not in accordance with service contract;
     •   Count IV: 46 U.S.C. § 41104(a)(3) (now § 41102(d)), retaliation; and
     •   Count V: 46 U.S.C. § 41104(a)(10), refusal to deal.

        OOCL contends that BBBY’s amended complaint consists of two general categories:
complaints about service and prices under its service contracts, and complaints about the
charges it incurred for demurrage and detention. OOCL argues that none of the claims have
“any legal merit, because the Commission has no authority to adjudicate these claims; nor do
any of the claims have factual merit, because OOCL neither breached its contractual obligations
to BBBY nor acted unreasonably.” Opposition at 1.

        BBBY was one of the largest home furnishing store chains in the United States before
declaring bankruptcy in 2023. OOCL is one of the largest ocean common carriers. BBBY and
OOCL entered into a series of service contracts agreeing to minimum quantity commitments
(“MQC”) and prices. A service contract is a reciprocal commitment between shipper and carrier
that ensures the carrier receives a certain volume of cargo and the shipper receives
commitments to certain space or rates. 46 U.S.C. § 40102(21). This provides stability to the
market. In contrast, spot market rates can fluctuate significantly. When the spot rates rose
during the COVID pandemic, BBBY alleges that OOCL failed to meet its space and price
commitments and instead provided space to the highest bidder, what BBBY describes as price
gouging. 3 Moreover, BBBY alleges it was charged excessive detention and demurrage fees, that
OOCL refused to deal, and that OOCL retaliated against it.

        “A carrier must establish a reasonable plan in order to cope with periods of congestion
and must fill its capacity in a reasonable and just manner when such periods occur.” Levantino
& Sons v. Prudential-Grace Lines, 18 F.M.C. 89, 104 (ALJ Aug. 17, 1973) (admin. final Dec.
16, 1974). 4 This initial decision relies on long-standing Commission precedent, which holds that
where the demand for space exceeds the supply, a common carrier must equitably apportion its
available space among shippers. Boston Wool Trade Assoc. v. Merchants Miners Transp. Co.,
Docket No. 11, 1 U.S.S.B. 32, 34 (USSB Dec. 13, 1921) (“apportioned as equitably as
possible,” not just to increase revenue); Grace Line Inc. v. FMB, 280 F.2d 790, 793 (2d Cir.
1960) (“if a shipper qualified, he may not be denied space at the pleasure, and for the profit, of
the carrier”); California Shipping Line, Inc. v. Yangming Marine Transp. Corp., Docket No. 88-
15, 1990 WL 427466, at *32 (FMC Oct. 19, 1990) (“carriers lacking sufficient space must pro-
rate their available space among contract shippers”). Moreover, an efficient and economic
transportation system “‘is one in which all parties adhere to agreed-upon responsibilities.’”
Bakerly, LLC v. Seafrigo USA, Inc., Docket No. 22-17, 2026 WL 254020, at *18 (FMC Jan. 22,
2026) (attorney fees order) (quoting Edaf Antillas, Inc. v. Crowley Caribbean Logistics, Docket
No. 14-04, 2016 WL 5110081, *13 (FMC Sept. 14, 2016)).

        As explained below, the evidence shows that OOCL violated section 41102(c) with
regard to its service commitments as alleged in Count I and violated the filed rate doctrine,
section 41104(a)(2), as alleged in Count III. Moreover, the evidence shows that OOCL refused
to deal as alleged in Count V and retaliated against BBBY as alleged in Count IV. However, the

         BBBY seeks damages of $165,355,872.69. OOCL objects to BBBY’s damages
calculations and some of OOCL’s concerns are well-founded. Reparations are awarded for
failure to meet the initial space and price commitments. No additional reparations are awarded

awarded is $45,600,599.25.
