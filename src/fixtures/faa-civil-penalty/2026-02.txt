                       ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR EXTENSION

    Complainant timely filed a notice of appeal in this matter on March 17, 2026,
following Administrative Law Judge J.E. Sullivan’s March 9, 2026, service of her
initial decision. 1

   On March 26, 2026, Complainant moved for a stay to file its appeal brief and to
hold the appeal in abeyance pending the outcome of a separate, earlier-filed appeal
involving a different respondent. 2 Complainant served Respondent with a copy of its
motion on the same day, after not receiving a response to its pre-motion
communication efforts with Respondent. 3

   Without an extension of time to perfect its appeal, Complainant’s appeal brief is
due no later than April 28, 2026.4 As a general matter, the rules do not contemplate
or provide for a stay to file briefings or a general stay of the appeal proceedings. 5
The rules, however, do provide for an extension of time to perfect an appeal without

   Absent agreement of all parties to extend the time to perfect an appeal, an
extension may be granted if the moving party demonstrates good cause. 7
Respondent did not file a response to Complainant’s motion within the ten-day
period to do so, therefore only Complainant’s motion is considered. 8

    Complainant details compelling reasons for an extension of its time to perfect its
appeal with an appeal brief. As Complainant states, a separate, earlier-filed appeal
presents substantially similar facts and the same legal issue as presented in this
matter. The outcome of that appeal will necessarily inform the parties and the
Office of Adjudication of the relevant legal standards under which this case will be
decided. Requiring briefing of this case before the earlier-filed appeal is decided will
likely result in a need for additional briefing. That likely result is inefficient for the
parties and this Office, adding unnecessary time and expense with little to no added
benefit to resolving this appeal.

    Moreover, there is no harm to Respondent. At present, Respondent has no civil
penalty liability resulting from Judge Sullivan’s initial decision. Not only will an
extension of time mitigate Respondent’s time and expenses to respond to
Complainant’s briefings, but there is some chance that the outcome of the earlier-
filed case will result in this appeal being withdrawn or otherwise resolved without
briefings, which would negate the need for any further time or expense. 9

   In sum, Complainant presents good cause for extending the time to perfect its
appeal. Complainant’s motion for an extension is GRANTED. Complainant’s time to
perfect its appeal is extended to 30 calendar days after Complainant’s office (FAA
Aviation Litigation Division, AGC-300) is served with the FAA Order deciding the
appeal 10 of Docket Number G13-22-040, unless the time to perfect is extended in
accordance with 14 CFR § 13.233(c). Respondent may then file a reply brief not
later than 35 calendar days after being served with Complainant’s appeal brief,
unless the time to reply is extended in accordance with 14 CFR § 13.233(e).
