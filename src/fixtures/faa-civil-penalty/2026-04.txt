    ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE AMICUS CURIAE BRIEF
               AND LEAVE FOR PARTY RESPONSE BRIEFS

    On June 30, 2026, the United States Department of Homeland Security,
Transportation Security Administration (TSA), moved for leave to submit an amicus
curiae brief in this matter. TSA explains that it has an interest in the outcome of
the matter because the administrative law judge’s determination on the complaint
filed against Respondent rendered legal conclusions regarding the delineation of
enforcement authority between TSA and the FAA with respect to interference with
crew members. Additionally, TSA highlights that the FAA decisionmaker’s
determination in this appeal may involve analysis of TSA regulations.

   The FAA decisionmaker has discretion to allow an amicus curiae brief in
appeals. 1 A non-party may be afforded an opportunity to submit an amicus curiae
brief when they demonstrate a substantial interest that is not represented by the
parties or that such a brief is otherwise necessary for a proper disposition of the
case. 2

   TSA’s motion is well taken. The appealed action by a Department of
Transportation administrative law judge interprets and makes conclusions on TSA
statutory authorities and the interplay between its and the FAA’s enforcement
authorities. TSA has a substantial interest in ensuring that its statutory and
regulatory authorities are correctly interpreted and there are no gaps in
enforcement related to aviation and transportation security. While Complainant’s
counsel can present its perspectives and arguments on legal authorities relating to
other agencies, TSA is uniquely positioned to provide perspectives and arguments
on its own legal authorities. Moreover, a brief by the agency whose statutes or
authorities are being interpreted, or by the Department of Justice as counsel to such
