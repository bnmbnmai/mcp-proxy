               ORDER GRANTING COMPLAINANT'S MOTION TO DISMISS
  Complainant filed a Motion to Dismiss, arguing that Respondent Michael
Bennett failed to file a timely and sufficient notice of appeal. I grant the motion and
dismiss this matter.

I. Background
    Complainant charged Respondent Michael Bennett with several regulatory
violations for improper operation of a small, unmanned aircraft system and sought
a civil penalty of $2,200.1 Mr. Bennett requested a hearing. Following several cross                     -




motions for summary judgment and Mr. Bennett's waiver of the right to a
testimonial hearing, Administrative Law Judge (AU) J.E. Sullivan issued the
Initial Decision assessing an $800 civil penalty.2 At the end of the Initial Decision,
the AU advised both parties that the "Initial Decision shall be considered a final
order unless one of the parties files a notice of appeal within 10 days of service of this
Initial Decision."3 The Initial Decision was served on February 4, 2025.

    Neither party filed a notice of appeal within the 10 days following service of the
Initial Decision. But on Saturday, February 15, 2025-11 days after service of the
Initial Decision-Mr. Bennett sent an email (Email) without attachments to the FAA

II. Discussion
    Complainant raises two basic grounds for its Motion to Dismiss. First, it argues
that the Email is not a notice of appeal because it does not conform to the email
filing requirements found in 14 CFR § 13.210. Second, it argues that if the Email is
treated as a notice of appeal, it is untimely under 14 CFR § 13.233(a).8 Each issue
and Mr. Bennett's responses are discussed below.

   A. Email messages alone are not acceptable filings.
   Under 14 CFR § 13.210, a party may file documents in an administrative civil
penalty case using U.S. Mail, a courier, fax, or email.9 Paragraph (h) of the
regulation explains filing by email:

              (h) Filing by email A document that is filed by email must be attached
              as a PDF file to an email. The document must be signed in accordance
              with § 13.207. The email message does not constitute a submission, but
              serves only to deliver the attached PDF file to the FAA Hearing
              Docket.
A notice of appeal that does not conform to this regulation may be accepted when a
complainant does not object.'° But here, Complainant objects and seeks dismissal.

Accordingly, I find the Email is not a notice of appeal.

   To fall within the good cause exception, Mr. Bennett argues without support that
the regulation refers to business days rather than calendar days.2° He also suggests
that Lincoln's Birthday (February 12) and perhaps Valentine's Day (February 14)
were Federal holidays.2' He speculates (his term) that Complainant's counsel and
other FAA employees were not "in their offices" on those days and admits that he
"certainly was not thinking of [his] legal matter on those days."22

   None of Mr. Bennett's arguments establish good cause. Section 13.212 addresses
the computation of time and when additional time for weekends and Federal
holidays is provided:

             (b) The date of an act, event, or default is not included in a
             computation of time under this subpart.

             (c) The last day of a time period is included unless it is a Saturday,
             Sunday, or a Federal holiday. If the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or
             Federal holiday, the time period runs until the end of the next day that
             is not a Saturday, Sunday, or Federal holiday.23

Under (b), the date of service of the Initial Decision (February 4, 2025) is not
included in the 10 days for filing the notice of appeal. Paragraph (c) gives additional
time oniy if the tenth day lands on a weekend or Federal holiday. Other weekends
and holidays are not considered. Thus, the deadline is computed using calendar
days, i.e., without considering intervening weekends or Federal holidays.

   Applying the regulation, Mr. Bennett's deadline to file a notice of appeal was
February 14, 2025, i.e., Valentine's Day. Federal holidays, defined in 5 U.S.C.
§ 6103, do not include Valentine's Day.24 Therefore, the deadline for filing a notice of
appeal remained Friday, February 14, 2025.

   Having found that AU Sullivan informed Mr. Bennett of the 10 -day deadline
and that good cause for missing the deadline is not present, I conclude that the
Email message was filed too late to preserve Mr. Bennett's right to an
administrative appeal.

   C. The rules apply to unrepresented parties.
      I am mindful that Mr. Bennett elected not to engage an attorney to represent
him. But the procedural rules apply to all parties, even those without counsel.25
Moreover, Mr. Bennett's lack of counsel does not grant him special treatment
because he is uninformed about basic procedures and opted not to familiarize
himself with the applicable rules.

   The law and facts support this view.26 At the beginning of the case before the
AU, the Notice of Assignment and Order described procedures for emailing PDFs
or using alternative filing methods.27 At the end of the case, the Initial Decision
provided the appeal deadline. Finally, Mr. Bennett admits to not thinking about his
appeal on Valentine's Day and is unwilling to "spend all [his] time looking up
regulations."28 Thus, enforcing the procedural rules in his case is not unjust.

III. Conclusion
    As explained above, Mr. Bennett's Email does not constitute a notice of appeal.
Further, even if it could be construed as such, Mr. Bennett has not shown good
cause for submitting it after the required deadline. Accordingly, Complaint's motion
is granted, and this matter is DISMISSED.
