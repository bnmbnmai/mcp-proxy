         1.     The Commission approves the attached Stipulation and Consent Agreement
         (Agreement) between the Office of Enforcement and Regulatory Accounting
         (Enforcement) and Interstate Power and Light Company (Interstate). This order is in the
         public interest because the Agreement resolves on fair and equitable terms Enforcement’s
         investigation (Investigation) under Part 1b of the Commission’s regulations, 18 C.F.R.
         Part 1b (2025), into whether Alliant Energy Corporate Services, Inc. (AECS), acting on
         the behalf of Interstate, misrepresented to Potomac Economics (Potomac), the market
         monitor for the Midcontinent Independent System Operator, Inc. (MISO), the operational
         status of the Marshalltown Combustion Turbine generating station (MTown CT) in
         connection with a routine audit performed by Potomac in December 2023 and January
         2024 (the Relevant Period).

         2.     Interstate stipulates to the facts in Section II of the Agreement but it neither admits
         nor denies the alleged violations described in Section III of the Agreement. Interstate
         agrees to: (a) pay a civil penalty of $70,000 to the United States Treasury; and (b) be
         subject to compliance monitoring as provided more fully below.

         I.     Facts

         3.   The MTown CT1 is a three-unit natural gas fired combustion turbine that
         commenced operations in 1978. Each combustion turbine unit has a capacity rating of
         63 MW. MTown CT is owned and operated by Interstate.2

         4.    AECS is responsible for bidding each of the MTown CT Units into MISO’s
         annual Planning Resource Auction (PRA). This includes MISO’s 2023-24 PRA, which
         occurred on April 21, 2023.

         5.     MISO’s 2023-24 PRA was the first to implement MISO’s seasonal market design.
         Under this construct, resources like MTown CT were bid based on their seasonal
         availability and resources that clear the PRA are, in turn, subject to seasonal performance
         requirements.3 Where resources are unable to meet those requirements due to prolonged
         outages they are generally required to procure replacement capacity or pay Capacity
         Replacement Non-Compliance Charges (CRNCC Charges).4 MISO’s Tariff imposes
         CRNCC Charges on capacity resources whose outages “were not planned but were
         known or could have been reasonably anticipated at the time of the PRA . . . .”5

         6.      In the Summer and Fall seasons of the 2023–24 PRA, AECS offered in the full
         capacity of MTown CT Unit 1 (one of the three combustion turbine units operating at
         Marshalltown). When those seasons came to pass, however, MTown CT Unit 1 was only
         partially available due to derates caused by equipment failures.

         7.     On December 12, 2023, Potomac e-mailed AECS indicating that Potomac was
         conducting an “audit” of “outages associated” with MTown CT Unit 1 (the Audit
         Inquiry). Specifically, Potomac sought information concerning a forced outage ticket for
         MTown CT Unit 1 that was dated in December 2022. The Audit Inquiry indicated that
         Potomac was looking for information concerning the underlying “equipment failure” and
         any “plans for repair” to the unit.

         8.     The Audit Inquiry further stated that Potomac was acting in its capacity as MISO’s
         Independent Market Monitor and contained in the body of the email the text of Section
         35.41(b) of the Commission’s regulations, which prohibits anyone authorized under the
         Federal Power Act to make sales for resale of electric energy, capacity, or ancillary
         services at market-based rates to make a false or misleading statement to, among others,
         Commission-approved market monitors, such as Potomac.

         9.      After the Audit Inquiry was received, AECS employees began preparing a
         response. Contemporaneous communications indicate that those employees were aware
         of the relationship between Potomac’s inquiry into the prolonged derate at Marshalltown
         Unit 1 and the possibility of CRNCC Charges.

         10.    On December 20, 2023, AECS employees provided their response to the Audit
         Inquiry (the Audit Response). In the Audit Response, AECS made specific factual
         representations concerning MTown CT Unit 1 and the circumstances around its return to
         service.

         11.     AECS explained in the Audit Response how the December 2022 forced outage
         ticket that was the subject of the Audit Inquiry was linked to an earlier forced outage at
         MTown CT Unit 1 that occurred in August 2022.

         12.    AECS also stated in the Audit Response that, between August 2022 and May
         2023, AECS had taken steps to identify a vendor to perform repairs on MTown CT Unit
         1 and during that period had vetted a vendor that could provide those services.

         13.   AECS represented to Potomac that at some point between May 2023 and
         September 2023, a new forced outage occurred at MTown CT Unit 3 (another of the three
         combustion turbine units operating at Marshalltown).

         14.    AECS also explained in the Audit Response that AECS decided to prioritize
         repairs to MTown CT Unit 3 using the vendor that had been vetted to repair MTown CT
         Unit 1, which could only repair one turbine at a time.

         15.    AECS went on to describe in the Audit Response how the decision to prioritize
         repairs to MTown CT Unit 3, and other issues, delayed MTown CT Unit 1’s return to
         service.

         16.   On December 20, 2023, AECS sent the Audit Response to Potomac. The AECS
         employee who prepared the Audit Response provided a copy of the response to a
         supervisor but did not obtain supervisory review of its contents.

         17.   On December 27, 2023, Potomac provided AECS a series of follow-up questions
         concerning the Audit Response.

         18.     On January 10, 2024, AECS provided Potomac with a new timeline and
         description of events (the Revised Response), which both revealed and corrected several
         false statements in the Audit Response.

         19.   Although AECS’s Audit Response indicated MTown CT Unit 3 had suffered a
         new outage in or later than May 2023, in the Revised Response AECS explained that in
         fact MTown CT Unit 3 had been significantly derated since July 2022.

         20.    In its Revised Response, AECS explained that it had sent damaged components
         for MTown CT Unit 3 to a vendor for repairs in November 2022. In September 2023, the
         vendor released MTown CT Unit 3 back to AECS and AECS placed it into service. That
         same month AECS began the process of securing funding and attempted to vet an
         alternative vendor for MTown CT Unit 1 but subsequently determined to replace MTown
         CT Unit 1 in November 2023. AECS secured funding and purchased a new unit in
         November 2023.

         21.    The representations AECS made in the Audit Response were generally consistent
         with a timeline that would reduce the likelihood of the imposition of CRNCC Charges
         because they suggested a May 2023 (or later) outage at MTown CT Unit 3 contributed to
         a delay in MTown CT Unit 1’s return to service. However, in light of AECS’s
         corrections in the Revised Response, Potomac went on to recommend CRNCC Charges
         be assessed against Interstate arising from the availability status of MTown CT Unit 1
         during the Summer 2023 and Fall 2023 seasons. Those charges subsequently were
         assessed against, and paid by, Interstate.

         22.     The AECS employee who prepared and submitted the Audit Response to Potomac
         on behalf of Interstate failed to exercise sufficient care in preparing the Audit Response;
         failed to make use of data sources available to them to confirm the accuracy of the Audit

         33.     Interstate agrees to pay a civil penalty of $70,000 to the United States Treasury, by
         wire transfer, within ten days after the Effective Date of this Agreement, as defined

                B.     Compliance

         34.    Interstate shall make an annual compliance monitoring report to Enforcement for a
         term of two years after the Effective Date of this Agreement. The first compliance
         monitoring report shall be submitted no later than 30 days after the end of the one-year
         reporting period that follows the Effective Date of this Agreement. The second compliance
         monitoring report shall be due one year after the first compliance monitoring report was
         submitted. After the receipt of that second compliance report, Enforcement may, at its sole
         discretion, require Interstate to submit a compliance monitoring report for one additional
         year.

         35.    Each compliance monitoring report shall: (1) identify any known violations of
         Commission regulations and/or Commission-approved tariff requirements that occurred
         during the applicable period, including a description of the nature of the violation and what
         steps were taken to rectify the situation; (2) describe all compliance measures and
         procedures Interstate instituted or modified during the reporting period related to
         compliance with Commission regulations and/or Commission-approved tariff
         requirements, including but not limited to new or updated compliance measures and
         procedures related to the facts and circumstances described in Section II; and (3) describe
         all Commission-related compliance training(s) that Interstate administered during the
         reporting period, including the dates such training(s) occurred, the topics covered, and the
         procedures used to confirm which personnel attended.

         36.    Each compliance monitoring report shall also include an affidavit executed by an
         officer of Interstate stating that it is true and accurate to the best of his/her knowledge.

         37.  Upon request by Enforcement, Interstate shall provide to Enforcement
         documentation supporting the contents of its reports.
