         1.      On October 24, 2025, ExxonMobil Oil Corporation (EMOC) filed a complaint
         (Complaint) requesting that the Commission direct LOCAP LLC (LOCAP) to follow its
         tariff or require modifications to LOCAP’s tariff. For the reasons discussed below, we
         deny the Complaint.

         I.    Background

         2.     EMOC is a shipper on the LOCAP pipeline system. EMOC states that it operates
         a Gulf Coast refinery at Baton Rouge (Baton Rouge Refinery). EMOC explains that the
         Baton Rouge Refinery processes crude petroleum that is transported on LOCAP from
         U.S. offshore production, sometimes referred to as Mars crude.1

         3.     EMOC states that in mid-June 2025, its Baton Rouge Refinery experienced upset
         conditions on the desalter and low pH outside of the typical operations envelope of the
         equipment. EMOC states that when it observed these upset conditions, it began testing
         crude oil received from the LOCAP system for metals and that tests identified elevated
         levels of zinc. EMOC explains that further testing of crude samples from earlier in June

         II.    Complaint

         5.      EMOC argues that LOCAP did not follow its tariff in its response to EMOC’s
         communication that delivered crude petroleum was not merchantable. EMOC requests
         that the Commission direct LOCAP to abide by its tariff and respond expeditiously to
         shipper notifications that the crude petroleum it receives is not merchantable.

         6.      EMOC argues that LOCAP’s tariff prohibits the transportation of crude petroleum
         shipped on the pipeline that materially affects the quality of the delivered product.5
         EMOC argues that Items No. 5 and 10 of the LOCAP tariff provide that LOCAP will
         only transport petroleum, which includes only “the direct liquid product of oil wells, or a
         mixture of the direct liquids products of oil wells with the indirect liquid products of oil
         and gas wells including gasoline and liquefied petroleum gases.”6 EMOC further states
         that zinc is not a direct product of oil wells, and that therefore, LOCAP’s tariff
         specifically excludes the shipment of zinc or any zinc derivatives from transportation on
         the LOCAP system.

         7.     EMOC states that Item No. 15 of the LOCAP tariff also prohibits the
         transportation of zinc because it requires that crude petroleum tendered for shipment be
         merchantable. According to EMOC, the presence of zinc rendered the shipments at issue
         not merchantable because it materially affected the quality of other shipments of crude

         12.     In its answer, LOCAP argues that the Complaint fails to establish any wrongdoing
         by LOCAP. LOCAP argues that the tariff does not prohibit or restrict the presence of
         zinc. While Item No. 5 limits transportation on the pipeline to “petroleum,” LOCAP
         rejects EMOC’s argument that zinc is outside the tariff’s definition of petroleum and
         therefore disallowed. LOCAP argues that notwithstanding the presence of any zinc, the
         petroleum in question was within the tariff’s definition because it was a “direct liquid
         product of oil wells.”

         13.     LOCAP also argues that the zinc-contaminated crude petroleum is not prohibited
         by Item No. 15 of the tariff, which requires that shippers tender merchantable crude
         petroleum with a specific gravity and temperature and not more than 2% of sediment,
         water, and other impurities.13 LOCAP emphasizes that under Item No. 15, it is the
         shipper’s duty to tender merchantable crude petroleum, and by tendering crude petroleum
         to LOCAP, a shipper implicitly affirms that the shipment meets the tariff’s requirements.
         Further, LOCAP argues its tariff does not require LOCAP to determine whether the crude
         petroleum a shipper tenders is merchantable or meets a shipper’s specifications.
         Moreover, LOCAP states that the merchantability standard indicates that LOCAP will not
         receive crude oil from a shipper if that crude oil materially affects “the quality of other
         shipments or cause[s] disadvantage to other shippers and/or the Carrier.” Therefore,
         LOCAP argues that the merchantability requirement is satisfied if a shipment does not
         materially affect other shippers or the carrier. However, the merchantability requirement
         is not a guarantee that a shipment of crude petroleum will be suitable for any particular
         shipper’s commercial purposes. LOCAP states that in this instance, no other shipper was
         harmed or disadvantaged as a result of EMOC’s shipment, as EMOC’s crude petroleum
         was not commingled with any other crude petroleum while being transported by
         LOCAP.14

         14.    Further, LOCAP states that the tariff does not require LOCAP to test shipments of

         done to determine “the actual amount of suspended basic sediment, water, and other
