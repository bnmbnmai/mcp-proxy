         1.     On April 1, 2026, Saguaro Connector Pipeline, L.L.C. (Saguaro) filed a motion
         requesting a three-year extension of time, until February 15, 2030, to construct and place
         into service its Border Facility, extending from the United States to Mexico. For the
         reasons discussed below, we grant the extension request.

         I.     Background

         2.      On February 15, 2024, the Commission issued an order granting Saguaro
         authorization pursuant to section 3 of the Natural Gas Act (NGA), as well as a
         Presidential Permit, authorizing it to construct and operate a natural gas pipeline and
         related facilities for the export of natural gas from the United States to Mexico extending
         approximately 1,000 feet from a point in Hudspeth County, Texas to the center of the
         Rio Grande River (Border Facility).1 On the Mexican side of the border, the Border
         Facility pipeline will connect with the Sierra Madre Pipeline, that will extend to a
         liquified natural gas (LNG) export facility (MXP Project) under development by Mexico
         Pacific Limited LLC (MPL) on the West Coast of Mexico.2 The Authorization Order
         required the project to be placed in service by February 15, 2027.

         3.     On April 1, 2026, Saguaro filed a request for a three-year extension of time, until
         February 15, 2030, to complete construction of the project and make it available for
         service. Saguaro states that construction of the project has been delayed due to the

         III.   Discussion

         5.      The completion date specified in a certificate or authorization order provides what
         the Commission believes—based on the applicant’s initial project schedule and the
         Commission’s assessment of circumstances relevant to the specific project—to be a
         reasonable period of time for the project sponsor to complete construction and make the
         project available for service.11 However, construction deadlines may be extended for
         good cause.12 One way good cause can be shown is by the project sponsor demonstrating
         that it made good faith efforts to meet its deadline but encountered circumstances beyond
         its control.13 We consider extension requests on a case-by-case basis.14

         6.      Saguaro argues that good cause exists to grant an extension of time. The company
         states that it deferred reaching a final investment decision and the commencement of
         construction activities due to uncertainty regarding the outcome of now-resolved legal
         challenges to the Authorization Order.15 Saguaro emphasizes that since the D.C. Circuit
         issued a decision resolving the litigation, it has engaged in negotiations with MPL and
         other shippers that are interested in utilizing the project.16 The company maintains that it
         needs additional time to secure financing, negotiate with interested shippers, and
         coordinate the development of the Border Facility with the MXP Project.17 Saguaro
