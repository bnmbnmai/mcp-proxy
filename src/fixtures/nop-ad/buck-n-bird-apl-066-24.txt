           This Decision responds to an Appeal (APL-066-24) of a Notice of Proposed Suspension

of Certification under the National Organic Program (NOP) issued to Buck ‘N’ Bird (Buck) of

Nottingham, Pennsylvania by USDA accredited certifier Global Organic Alliance, Inc (GOA).

The operation has been deemed not in compliance with the Organic Foods Production Act of

1990 (Act) 1 and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) organic regulations. 2

                                            BACKGROUND

           The Act authorizes the Secretary to accredit agents to certify crop, livestock, wild crop,

and/or handling operations to the USDA organic regulations (7 CFR part 205). Certifying agents

also initiate compliance actions to enforce program requirements, as described in section

205.662, Noncompliance procedure for certified operations. The NOP may also initiate

compliance actions against operations and certifying agents to enforce program requirements.

Persons subject to the Act who believe they are adversely affected by a noncompliance decision

of a certifying agent, or the NOP may appeal such decision to the USDA Agricultural Marketing

Service (AMS) pursuant to § 205.680 Adverse Action Appeals Process – General, and §

                                     FINDINGS OF FACT

   1. On September 10, 2021, GOA certified Buck for crops and livestock.

   2. On March 11, 2024, GOA issued Buck a Notice of Noncompliance for failure to provide

       input and seed records, use of prohibited substances, use of inputs not listed in its

       Organic System Plan, use of nonorganic seed, use of livestock feed not approved by

       GOA, and inadequate documentation to support dry matter percentage used for livestock.

   3. On May 30, 2024, GOA issued Buck a Notice of Proposed Suspension because Buck did

       not resolve the previous noncompliance.

   4. On June 11, 2024, Buck filed an Appeal of the Notice of Proposed Suspension to NOP

       and requested additional time to find a new certifier.

   5. On December 4, 2024, Buck entered into a Settlement Agreement with NOP whereby,

       among other things, Buck agreed to maintain input documentation for all seed purchases

       to include whether seeds were untreated/non-genetically modified organism (GMO),

       receive certifier approval prior to use of inputs for its organic livestock and organic crops,

       and ensure ruminant livestock obtain a minimum of 30 percent of the livestock’s dry

       matter intake from pasture during the grazing season.

   6. Buck applied for organic certification with Quality Certification Services (QCS). On June

       24, 2025, QCS issued Buck a Notice of Noncompliance and Denial of Certification for

       non-correctable violations and failure to implement corrective actions to similar

       noncompliances previously issued by GOA. The violations QCS identified indicated that

       Buck had breached its Settlement Agreement with NOP.

                                 REGULATORY CITATIONS

       The USDA organic regulations at 7 CFR 205.400, General requirements for certification,

state that, “A person seeking to receive or maintain organic certification under the regulations in

this part must: (a) Comply with the Act and applicable organic production and handling

regulations of this part …

       The regulations at 7 CFR 205.105 state that products sold or labeled as “organic” or

“made with organic” must be produced and handled without the use of synthetic substances and

ingredients except as provided in 7 CFR 205.601 or 7 CFR 205.603, or nonsynthetic substances

prohibited in 7 CFR 205.602 or 7 CFR 205.604. In addition, 7 CFR 205.105(e) states that

organic products must be produced without the use of excluded methods, except for vaccines

approved in accordance with 7 CFR 205.606. “Excluded methods” are defined in 7 CFR 205.2 as

methods used to genetically modify organisms or influence their growth and development by

means that are not possible under natural conditions or processes and are not considered

compatible with organic production.

       The regulations at 7 CFR 205.201(a) state that an organic production or handling system

plan must include a list of each substance to be used as a production or handling input, indicating

its composition, source, location(s) where it will be used, and documentation of commercial

availability, as applicable. Additionally, an organic production or handling plan must include a


                                            Page 3 of 9
description of the management practices and physical barriers established to prevent

commingling of organic and nonorganic products on a split operation and to prevent contact of

organic production and handling operations and products with prohibited substances.

       The regulations at 7 CFR 205.204(a)(1) state that the producer must use organically

grown seeds, annual seedlings, and planting stock, except that non organically produced,

untreated seeds and planting stock may be used to produce an organic crop when an equivalent

organically produced variety is not commercially available. However, organically produced seed

must be used for the production of edible sprouts.

       The regulations at 7 CFR 205.237(a) state that the producer of an organic livestock

operation must provide livestock with a total feed ration composed of agricultural products,

including pasture and forage, that are organically produced and handled. In addition, 7 CFR

205.237(d) states that ruminant livestock producers shall (1) describe the total feed ration for

each type and class of animal. The description must include the percentage of each feed type,

including pasture, in the total ration; and (4) provide the method for calculating dry matter

demand and dry matter intake.

                                          DISCUSSION

       Global Organic Alliance, Inc (GOA) certified Buck for crops and livestock on September

10, 2021.

       On March 11, 2024, GOA issued Buck a Notice of Noncompliance. According to the

Notice, Buck did not provide sufficient records to confirm that the active ingredient, citric acid,

found in anti-diarrheal medicine Buck administered to its organic livestock, was not derived

from genetically modified products; a violation of the USDA organic regulations at 7 CFR

205.105.

       Further, Buck did not provide documentation to support that it was using inputs and seeds

that were labeled and stored to prevent commingling and to protect organic products from

contact with prohibited substances. Buck also did not list inputs for its crop or livestock in its

Organic System Plan. These are violations of the USDA organic regulations at 7 CFR

205.204(a)(1), and 7 CFR 205.201(a), respectively.
