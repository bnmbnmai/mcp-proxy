                                        SUMMARY

        The Federal Land Policy and Management Act (FLPMA) and its implementing
regulations require BLM to charge, and ROW holders to pay, fair market value annually
for use of the public lands encumbered by a ROW. For ROWs used to accommodate
support facilities for solar energy development projects, BLM charges the ROW holder an
acreage rent. Departmental regulations provide that these acreage rents are generally
calculated using a defined set of inputs and formulas, but they also provide for
adjustments and alternative approaches where application of the rent schedule derived
from those calculations does not yield an appropriate fair market value. Here, Appellants
assert that the schedule-based rental rates for their three ROWs exceed fair market value,
in violation of FLPMA, and they provide both data from their own real estate
transactions in the area and two third-party professional valuation reports to support
their claims. BLM largely relies on its adherence to the default regulatory formulas to
insulate the resulting rental charges from challenge, and while it criticizes Appellants’
support, it offers no record support of its own. Based on the record on appeal, we find
that Appellants have met their burden to demonstrate that BLM has not adequately
justified its rejection of Appellants’ request for an alternative, appraisal-based approach
to determining fair market value rentals for their ROWs, in the face of the proffered
support indicating that the schedule-based rents were inconsistent with FLPMA’s
mandate to charge fair market value as applied to the lands at issue. Accordingly, we set
aside the appealed acreage rent bills and remand to BLM for reconsideration of its
assessments in light of the record and this Decision.
