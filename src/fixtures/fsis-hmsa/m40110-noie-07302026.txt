FSIS is taking this action in accordance with Title 9 of the Code Federal Regulations (CFR) Rules of
Practice 500.3(b), based on the determination that Evergen handled or slaughtered livestock
inhumanely, in violation of the Federal Meat Inspection Act (FMIA) (21 U.S.C. 601 et seq.), the
Humane Methods of Slaughter Act (HMSA) (7 U.S.C. 1901 ef seq.), and the relevant regulatory
requirements (9 CFR Part 313).
This letter provides you with information on the Agency’s authority, the facts supporting FSIS’ action,
and information on how you can respond.
Background/Authority
FSIS is the agency in the U.S. Department of Agriculture responsible for protecting the public’s health
by ensuring the nation's supply of meat, poultry, and processed egg products for sale and distribution
in commerce is safe, unadulterated, wholesome, and correctly labeled and packaged. FSIS administers
and enforces, among other laws, the FMIA, HMSA, and the statutes’ implementing regulations. FSIS
has prescribed rules and regulations required for establishments producing meat/meat food products,
including requirements pertaining to humane handling and slaughtering of livestock (9 CFR Part 313)
and other matters. FSIS has also developed the Rules of Practice regarding enforcement (9 CFR Part
500). The Rules of Practice describe the types of enforcement action that FSIS may take and include
procedures for taking a withholding action and/or suspension, with or without prior notification, and
for filing a complaint to withdraw a Federal Grant of Inspection.

Findings/Basis for Action
On July 29, 2026, at approximately 0751 hours, while performing HATS Category IX Task;
Consciousness on the Rail, IPP observed the following noncompliance:
The first electrical stun was administered to the head of a hog and it was immediately rendered
unconscious. Two additional electrical stuns were administered, one to the head and one on the chest
as part Evergen’s routine process, The hog was then shackled, hoisted, and cut. When the incision to
exsanguinate was made, the blood flow observed from the incision was substantially less than
normal.
During the transfer from the exsanguination area to the scalder, IPP observed the hog exhibit signs of
consciousness. IPP observed the animal tracking with its eyes, blinking, and attempting to right itself
while hoisted. Evergen noted the eye-tracking movements and blinking. Evergen immediately used a
handheld captive bolt device to render the animal unconscious. IPP verified the hog was unconscious
before dressing procedures continued.

notified of the regulatory control action and forthcoming noncompliance record for failure to meet
the regulatory requirements of 9 CFR 313.30(a)(4). This establishment was operating under a Robust
Systemic Approach to humane handling at the time of this incident.
Conclusion
On September 9, 2004, FSIS published "Humane Handling and Slaughter Requirements and the Merits
of a Systematic Approach to Meet Such Requirements" in the Federal Register (69 FR 54625).
Subsequently, on October 23, 2013, FSIS issued guidance, titled "FSIS Compliance Guide for a
Systematic Approach to the Humane Handling of Livestock," to promote a systematic approach to
achieve compliance with the FSIS requirements for the humane handling and slaughter of livestock. If
an establishment maintains a robust humane handling program, FSIS can exercise regulatory discretion
when an inhumane handling or slaughter incident is observed.
Based on the findings described above, Evergen handled or slaughtered livestock inhumanely, in
violation of the FMIA 21 U.S.C. 603(b), HMSA 7 U.S.C. 1901 — 1902, and 9 CFR 313.30(a)(4). FSIS
has decided to issue this Notice of Intended Enforcement in lieu of an immediate suspension based on
the details of the incident and Evergen’s prior implementation of a robust systematic approach to
humane handling.

How to Respond to this Letter
Please provide this office with a written response to this letter within three (3) business days from the
date of your receipt of this letter, no later than close of business August 3, 2026. FSIS will determine
further action, if any, based on your response. Your written response is expected to include:
¢ An evaluation and identification of the nature and cause of the incident.
e A description of the specific actions taken to eliminate the cause of the incident and prevent
future recurrences.
e Any corrective actions, preventative measures, and monitoring activities to ensure continued
compliance.
¢ Copies of supporting documentation and records maintained and/or associated with the
proposed corrective actions and preventative measures.
You may submit your response by electronic mail to, Aubrey. Williams@usda.gov,
fo/do25/desmoinesdistrictmanagementteam@usda.gov, and do25.humanehandling@usda.gov.
