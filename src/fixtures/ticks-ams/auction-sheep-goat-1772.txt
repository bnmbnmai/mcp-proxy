Compared to last sale: (8/31/26) Feeder lambs under 60 lbs sold sharply higher with most sales 21.00-25.00 higher, lambs 60-89 lbs sold
mostly steady to firm, lambs over 90 lbs sold fully steady. Demand was mostly good to very good for heavy offerings. Lamb quality this week
was average to very attractive and similar to last sale. Many long strings of lambs were seen this week and many were coming off range.
Lambs on offer today were in mostly light flesh and offered buyers good weigh conditions, bolstering demand. Several local farmer feeders
were in the stands again this sale and they showed very good demand for light weight lambs that they could slow grow until spring and
market for the spring religious holiday season. Demand was very consistent this sale as buyers competed over higher quality offerings and
stayed throughout the long sale which stretched into the evening. Very few wooled lambs were purchased for immediate harvest this sale as
feeding demand outpaced slaughter demand. Demand for light fleshed hair lambs suitable to feed was mostly good to very good. These
offerings sold mostly 20.00-30.00 higher. Most heavy weight hair lambs were purchased for slaughter this sale, these offerings sold mostly
10.00-15.00 lower than last sale. One attractive set of fed hair lambs were seen this week these offerings sold with mostly moderate demand.
Market activity this sale was active with rapid bidding noticed. Slaughter ewes sold on good to very good demand. Slaughter ewes sold fully
steady on good ewes, utility ewes sold mostly steady to 3.00 higher. Cull ewes were too lightly tested to develop any market trend. All goats
sold on good to very good demand. Feeding kids sold sharply higher with several ranchers in the stands actively bidding on lightweight kids to
feed. Feeding kids sold sharply higher with most sales 75.00-90.00 higher. Slaughter kids sold mostly 25.00-50.00 higher. Goat quality was
mostly average and overall not as attractive as last sale. Supply included: 52% Feeder Sheep/Lambs (90% Lambs, 10% Hair Lambs); 32%
Slaughter Sheep/Lambs (20% Hair Breeds, 74% Ewes, 2% Hair Ewes, 3% Rams, 0% Hair Rams); 0% Replacement Sheep/Lambs (100% Hair
Ewes); 9% Feeder Goats (100% Kids); 7% Slaughter Goats (53% Kids, 26% Nannies/Does, 7% Bucks/Billies, 14% Wethers).
