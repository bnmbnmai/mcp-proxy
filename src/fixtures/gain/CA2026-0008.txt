Report Highlights:

Production of oilseeds (including canola, soybeans, and sunflower seeds) is forecast to decrease four
percent in marketing year (MY) 2026/27 over the previous marketing year, to 27.55 million metric tons
(MMT). In February 2026, China announced that it will suspend all agricultural tariffs on Canadian
canola meal from March 1 through the end of 2026. On February 28, China announced that it will lower
its anti-dumping tariff on Canadian canola seed to 5.9 percent from the 75.8 percent imposed in August
2025. Canola oil continues to be subject to a tariff of 100 percent. Several regulatory changes are
expected to boost vegetable oil domestic consumption and exports in MY 2025/26 and MY 2026/27,
primarily for its use as a biofuel feedstock.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Oilseeds

Production of oilseeds (including canola, soybeans, and sunflower seeds) is forecast to decrease four
percent in marketing year (MY) 2026/27 over the previous marketing year, to 27.55 million metric tons
(MMT). This forecast is based on Statistics Canada’s survey-based seeding intentions data for MY
2026/27 and an assumption that yields of canola and sunflower seed will fall to their three-year average,
while soybean yields will increase to their three-year average. Area planted to canola is forecast to
increase one percent compared to the previous year; a return to average yields for canola is the largest
reason for the forecasted total oilseed production decline.

In MY 2026/27, total seed exports are forecast to increase seven percent on increased soybean exports
due to high demand for biofuel feedstocks in the United States. This is despite a 14 percent reduction in
canola seed exports due to increased domestic processing of canola seed as crush capacity rises from
13.56 MMT in 2025 to 14.8 MMT by the end of 2026. Imports of seed remains small due to Canada’s
limited capacity for soybean and sunflower seed processing, and its large domestic production of canola
seed.

In MY 2025/26, oilseed production increased seven percent over the previous marketing year to 28.67
MMT. Canola was the main driver in the increase, despite reduced area planted, owing to it having the
greatest yield gains.



Oil

In MY 2026/27, total oil production is forecast to increase by six percent over the previous year to reach
5.67 MMT, subsequently leading to increased domestic supply and a decline in vegetable oil imports
(mainly soybean oil). However, soybean oil imports (mainly from Argentina) are still expected to be
nearly 16 times larger than five years previous, due to demand for renewable fuel feedstock in
Newfoundland and Labrador. Canada will still hold a net positive trade balance in vegetable oil as soy
oil imports are still less than a third of canola oil exports.

Strong canola oil demand from the United States is expected to continue as demand for biofuel
feedstock in the United States increase due to large Renewable Volume Obligations (RVO) and
restrictions on the importation of foreign used cooking oil (UCO).

Canola oil exports will benefit from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) announcement
that it is expanding its renewable fuel volume obligations (RVOs) for 2026 and 2027. Further, canola
will benefit from EPA’s decision to delay a policy aimed to reduce RIN credits by 50 percent for fuels
produced from foreign feedstocks—such as used cooking oil (UCO) from China or canola oil from
Canada.

Canola Seed Production – Marketing Year (MY) 2026/2027

Statistics Canada’s March 5, 2026, Planting Intentions Report indicates that area planted to canola is
forecast to increase one percent over the previous marketing year. FAS/Ottawa forecasts that production
will fall by less than eight percent, assuming yields fall in line with the three-year average.

Saskatchewan remains the dominant producer, but increased area is expected across the Canadian
Prairies (Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Alberta) in response to growing crush capacity.

By the end of March, 39 percent of the country was classified as abnormally dry or in moderate to
severe drought (down from 53 percent a month earlier), including 32 percent of the country's agricultural
landscape (down from 60 percent a month earlier).

While it’s still early in the planting season, this data indicates that while the country is slightly drier than
a year ago, less agricultural area is currently impacted by drought. Last year, by the end of March 2025,
35 percent of the country was classified as abnormally dry or in moderate to severe drought, including
37 percent of the country’s agricultural landscape.

The war in Iran has impacted the global fertilizer market and will have a trickle-down impact on
Canada’s fertilizer market, as a reduction in supply drives up prices. This is particularly evident in
Ontario and Quebec, where most of the nitrogen supply is imported from outside of North America.
March 2026 analysis from Farm Credit Canada (FCC) warns that escalating nitrogen prices could
severely squeeze farm margins. It states that a hypothetical 40 percent rise in nitrogen costs could cut
average Saskatchewan crop margins for wheat and canola by half, from CDN $50 per acre to CDN $25
per acre, they state.

To date, the war in Iran has had minimal impact on Canada’s potash. Potash prices rose in early March
2026 but reacted less sharply to the war in Iran than nitrogen and phosphate fertilizers. As of the second
week of March 2026, the average retail price for potash in the U.S. reached U.S. $487 per ton, a slight
increase from approximately U.S. $485 per ton in late January.

New potash market entrant, Australian-owned BHP, is developing the Jansen Potash Mine near
Saskatoon, Saskatchewan, with a USD $10.3 billion investment. The project aims for initial production
by late 2026 or mid-2027, with a potential to become one of the world's largest potash mines, producing
up to 8.5 million tons annually.

Canola seed production – MY 2025/2026

Canadian canola production in MY 2025/26 achieved a record-breaking year, with production reaching
21.8 million metric tons (MMT), driven by record-high average yields of 2.507 MT/hectare. Despite
large production, producers reported lower harvested area in 2025 than the previous year.

Statistics Canada significantly revised upward their 2025 canola seed production estimate, a
continuation of a pattern of revisions as the agency transitions from model-based projections to farmer
survey data each December.



Canola seed exports – MY 2026/2027

Post forecasts that the volume of canola seed exported in MY 2026/2027 will fall from the previous
marketing year as a greater share of domestic supply is crushed within Canada. Regulatory uncertainty
around the canola oil export market in the United States has been resolved, at least for 2026 and 2027
(see the canola oil export section of this report for further details), which will boost the share of seed
supply going to domestic processing for canola oil export versus canola seed export. Further, Canada
will see increased crush capacity in 2026.

However, domestic processors may face increased competition for canola seed from Chinese canola
seed buyers due to a significant reduction in trade barriers that will allow for greater export volumes of
Canadian canola seed to China. On February 28, China announced it will lower its anti-dumping tariff
on Canadian canola seed to 5.9 percent from the 75.8 percent imposed in August 2025. The levy is
effective from March 1 and will last for at least five years. This announcement was expected after
Canada announced a preliminary deal with China on January 16, 2026.

Canola seed exports – MY 2025/2026

MY 2025/26 year-to-date (i.e. August to February), exports of canola seed are down 28 percent over the
same period of the previous year. This decrease is attributable to a significantly lower volume of exports
to China due to a 75.8 percent tariff imposed on Canada’s canola seed in August 2025. A second reason
for lower exports is the increased demand from domestic seed processors. Post forecasts that more than
37 percent of canola seed supply will be exported as raw product, compared to 42 percent in the
previous year, as domestic crush capacity increases. Improved certainty over U.S. Renewable
Identification Numbers, as explained in the canola oil section of this report, will give a further boost to
domestic processing and reduce the share of seeds that go to export.

Several factors will influence the seed and oil export balance in the second half of MY 2025/26 and
beyond. A strengthening Canadian dollar could make canola seed exports less competitive, unless
cancelled out by low canola prices. Secondly, seed prices remain low relative to four years ago when
they were nearly double average price in the first week of March 2026. Further, similar to the MY
2026/27 outlook, domestic processors may face increased competition for canola seed from Chinese
canola seed buyers due to a significant reduction in trade barriers that will allow for greater export
volumes of Canadian canola seed to China.

Price sensitive countries: FAS/Ottawa forecasts increased seed exports to price sensitive countries such
as United Arab Emirates (UAE), Bangladesh, and Pakistan, comparted to the previous year. In MY
2019/20, when canola prices were low, UAE, Bangladesh and Pakistan imported 20 percent of the total
share of Canadian canola seed exports. In MY 2023/24, when seed prices were higher, UAE imported
just two percent of the total share and Bangladesh and Pakistan did not import any.

Japan: Japan will likely sustain the same share of seed imports from Canada as it did last year.
Historically, Japan has relied on Canada for approximately 95-97 percent of its rapeseed imports.
However, starting in MY 2021/22, Japan increased the purchase of Australian canola seeds due to a poor
MY 2021/22 canola crop in Canada. Australia meanwhile had bumper canola crops in MY 2021/22 and
MY 2022/23. Japanese crushers also noted higher oil extraction rates from Australian canola seeds
compared to Canadian seeds. In MY 2022/23, only 38 percent of rapeseed imported to Japan came from
Canada and 62 percent came from Australia. Year to date (August to February), 72 percent of imports
were derived from Canada and 28 percent from Australia, similar to the previous marketing year.

European Union: Delivery volumes to the EU market have increased year to date (August to February)
over the same period of the previous year, mainly used for biofuel production. Because Canadian

farmers grow genetically modified varieties, the use of rapeseed oil derived from Canadian sources is
partly restricted in the EU. Canadian canola is a qualified feedstock for use in EU biofuels, but exporters
and growers need to be certified if they choose to take advantage of this opportunity, which reportedly
caused some bottlenecks in 2025.

China: China is an important market for Canada’s canola complex. In 2023, the last “normal” year of
exports for Canada’s canola complex, Canada exported 65 percent of its canola seed volume, 34 percent
of canola meal, and 3.7 percent of canola oil, to China, for a combined value of USD 3.7 billion.

But Canada’s canola sector has had a tumultuous relationship with China, as outlined in the text box that
follows. Most recently, in March 2025, China announced a 100 percent tariff rate on Canadian canola oil
and meal (along with other commodities), effective immediately. In August 2025, it announced
preliminary tariffs of 75.8 percent on canola seed.

On February 27, 2026, as expected by Canada, China announced that it will suspend all agricultural
tariffs on Canadian canola meal from March 1 through the end of 2026. On February 28, China
announced that it will lower its anti-dumping tariff on Canadian canola seed to 5.9 percent from the 75.8
percent imposed in August 2025. Canola oil continues to be subject to a tariff of 100 percent.

Canola seed imports

Canada has large domestic supplies of canola seed and is not a significant importer.

FAS/Ottawa is forecasting a 15 percent increase in canola seed imports in MY 2025/26, compared to the
previous marketing year, a nearly eight percent drop from the five-year (MY 20/21 to 24/25) average.
Market watchers speculate that the return to more “normal” import levels are likely due to the
improvement of the Canadian dollar.

Canada’s dollar value vis-à-vis the U.S. dollar remains weak by comparison to the five-year average but
has strengthened since 2025, and canola seed import prices have eased since the sustained high prices
that persisted for five months of 2025.

About 99 percent of Canada’s MY 2025/26 canola seed imports to date have come from the United
States, higher than the historical average.

Canola oil production - MY 2026/2027

FAS/Ottawa forecasts that 5,300 MT of canola oil will be produced in MY 2026/27, a 6.9 percent
increase over the previous year. This forecast assumes a three-year average (MY 2023/24 to MY
2025/26) oil extraction rate of 0.424 and a crush volume of 12,500 TMT canola seeds.

The Louie Dreyfus expansion in Yorkton, Saskatchewan is expected to come online in Fall 2026 and
add an additional 1.15 MMT, bringing its total capacity to 2.0 MMT, and bringing total national crush
capacity to 14.8 MMT.



Canola oil production - MY 2025/2026

Post forecasts that Canada will crush approximately 50 percent (11.7 MMT) of total canola supply
(where supply includes stocks, production, and imports), which is above the ten-year average of 9.8
MMT. From August to January (the most recent data available), Canada has already crushed 52 percent
of supply (including assumed imports), which is more than the three-year average of 50 percent for this
period.

Canada’s oilseed crush capacity is expected to grow from 13.56 MMT in 2025 to 14.81 MMT in
calendar year 2026, with the expansion of the Louis Dreyfus crush plant in Yorkton, Saskatchewan and
establishment of a new Cargill facility in Regina, Saskatchewan.

Cargill’s new crush facility in Regina, Saskatchewan is expected to begin commercial operation in

this marketing year (which runs from August to July), Post is forecasting that Canada’s crush capacity
will reach approximately 13.58 MMT over the entirety of MY 2025/26, up from 13.56 MMT at the
beginning of 2025.

Bunge and Viterra merged at the end of MY 2024/25, on July 2, 2025. The merger significantly impacts
Canadian canola processing by centralizing over 40 percent of the nation's crush capacity under one
firm, reducing competition within Canada. The merger also creates uncertainty regarding the
construction of new crush facilities, such as the proposed Viterra facility in Regina.



Canola oil exports - MY 2026/2027

Several factors are expected to boost foreign demand for Canadian canola oil to record high levels in
MY 2026/27.

Firstly, in March 2026, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) announced that it is expanding
its renewable fuel volume obligations (RVOs) for 2026 and 2027. Members of Canada’s canola industry
state that enlarged RVOs can’t be met by using only U.S. feedstocks, so the market for Canadian canola
is expected to expand. As a second part of this announcement, EPA stated that it is postponing changes
to how Renewable Identification Number (RIN) credits are applied to foreign feedstocks. Initially
proposed in June 2025 as part of the 2026–2027 Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) rules, the policy aimed
to reduce RIN credits by 50 percent for fuels produced from foreign feedstocks—such as used cooking
oil (UCO) from China or canola oil from Canada.

Secondly, legislative changes made to the United States’ One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA - Public
Law 119-21) on July 4, 2025, will benefit canola. For fuels produced after 2025, OBBBA limits the U.S.
Inflation Reduction Act 45Z Fuel Production Tax credit to fuel that is exclusively derived from a
feedstock that was produced or grown in the United States, Mexico, or Canada. This benefits Canadian
canola over other foreign feedstocks such as Chinese used cooking oil (UCO).1

Canadian canola oil is now the most prominent non-domestic beneficiary of the 45Z credit changes in
the OBBBA. Industry states that fifteen percent of the U.S. feedstock mix is canola oil in the best year,
including U.S. canola oil. The average over past ten years is about ten percent.

Further, OBBBA removed indirect land use change (ILUC) penalties from the calculation of emissions
for the 45Z tax credit, which allows canola oil’s carbon intensity score to fall low enough to enable it to
be used as a feedstock under 45Z.

However, there are also factors to watch for that could reduce Canadian canola oil exports.

Firstly, the forthcoming 2026 announcement of targeted amendments to the Clean Fuels Regulation
could include national domestic content requirements on biobased diesel production, resulting in more
biobased diesel facilities producing at capacity and consuming more domestic canola oil.

In April 2025, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) finalized amendments to the California Low
Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS). CARB proposed in August 2024 to limit virgin soy and canola oil to a 20
percent aggregate cap per company within the LCFS program. According to the American Soybean
Association, currently, virgin vegetable oils account for approximately 30 percent of the feedstock used
in California's biofuels market. CARB is essentially prioritizing used cooking oil and tallow over
vegetable oils, and this will lead to a reduction in canola oil use in California, as fuel suppliers adjust
their feedstock usage in the lead-up to the January 2028 implementation.

Separately, EPA stated that it aims to reduce RIN credits by 50 percent for fuels produced from foreign
feedstocks—such as used cooking oil (UCO) from China or canola oil from Canada, beginning
sometime in 2028. Details are forthcoming. In the lead-up to the implementation date, this news is
expected to drive increased investment in U.S. soybean crush capacity and boost U.S. domestic
feedstock demand.

Despite current free access for the oilseed complex (i.e. seed, oil and meal), the upcoming 2026
USMCA review creates potential for trade policy uncertainty.

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is projected to strengthen against the USD in 2026, with analysts
anticipating a rise towards 75 cents (USD). A stronger loonie, will likely make Canadian exports less
competitive than they were in 2026. A weak Canadian dollar, coupled with export prices that are
currently higher than in 2025 (e.g. canola oil and canola seed), could disadvantage net export volumes,
unless demand is strong enough.

Canola oil exports – 2025/2026

Year-to-date (August to February) exports are down nearly six percent over the same period in the
previous year, but this is primarily because exports to the United States were front loaded in MY
2024/25, ahead of the implementation of 45Z, whereas MY 2025/26 is expected to be back loaded.
FAS/Ottawa forecasts that the pace of demand for canola oil will increase in the second half of the
marketing year, raising total MY 2026/27 exports above the previous year’s. This increase is driven by
canola oil’s eligibility under 45Z as of July 2025, and EPA’s March 27, 2026 announcement of a final
rule to establish required Renewable Fuel Standard volumes and percentage standards for 2026 and
2027, which will lead to an increase of canola oil feedstocks in the remaining last few months of the
marketing year and into MY 2026/27.

Exports to the United States will be volatile throughout MY 2025/26 and have been since March 2024
(in the second half of MY 2023/24). Because exporters were concerned about the forthcoming details of
45Z, in March 2024 Canadian crushers began crushing at an increased capacity and exporting higher
volumes of oil. This period of increased exports continued into MY 2024/25, until it was announced in
January 10, 2025 that canola would be deemed ineligible for the 45Z tax credit due to its carbon
intensity score. 2 U.S. canola oil imports from Canada then declined drastically, falling 44 percent year-
over-year in the month of February 2025 and 42 percent year-over-year in March 2025. The temporary
shuttering of several biobased diesel facilities in the United States and Canada in MY 2024/25 may have
also contributed to the drop in canola oil exports.

Canola oil, imports

Canola oil imports remain small due to large domestic seed supplies and sufficient crush capacity. Of the
small volume imported, the United States and India account for approximately 74 percent and 10 percent,
respectively, with the remainder distributed across a range of other countries.



Canola oil, domestic consumption

Canola oil is used for food and industrial purposes, but official data does not disaggregate the two uses
by vegetable oil type. In addition to its use as a bio-based diesel (BBD, or biodiesel and renewable
diesel) feedstock, canola oil also has industrial uses in the production of paints and varnishes.

The vegetable oil and fuel trade dynamic has likely changed in Canada. Historically, Canadian canola
oil makes up the bulk of vegetable oil used to produce BBD within Canada, but not the bulk of
feedstock in BBD that is used in Canada. Until 2025, biodiesel produced in Canada was exported to the
United States and U.S. fuel suppliers benefitted from the (now expired) Blenders Tax Credit (BTC).
Meanwhile, the majority of BBD consumed in Canada was imported from the United States, produced
mainly from UCO, and some soy oil. This trade dynamic with the United States changed in January
2025 when the United States converted from having a blenders’ tax credit to having a producers’ tax
credit (45Z).



                                                                                                          18
Further, domestic content requirements on renewable fuels were implemented in Ontario and British
Columbia in 2025 in response to 45Z. The provinces adopted domestic production requirements due to
the Canadian federal government’s nonresponse to the U.S. Clean Fuel Production Tax Credit (45Z).
The 45Z tax credit established by the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) became available on January
1, 2025,3 and Canadian fuel suppliers lobbied the federal government to offer a production tax credit that
approximates 45Z. In absence of similar benefits, British Columbia and Ontario imposed domestic
content requirements. These requirements will presumably lead to more BBD being produced in Canada
than previously, larger volumes of canola oil being used in Canada, and more Canadian-produced BBD
remaining in Canada.

British Columbia requires that renewable content in diesel fuel be produced in Canada effective April 1,
2025 (and renewable content in gasoline be produced in Canada effective January 1, 2026). Further,
British Columbia has increased the renewable fuel requirement for diesel from four percent to eight
percent effective February 27, 2025 (the minimum renewable content requirement of gasoline remains at
five percent). Any volume above the eight percent can be imported from the United States. Whether
British Columbia imports renewable diesel feedstocks (such as soy oil or used cooking oil) from the
United States or uses Canadian canola oil will depend on relative feedstock pricing and, somewhat, the
carbon intensity scores.

On July 8, 2025, Ontario’s Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks announced its decision
to proceed with domestic bio-based content requirements for both diesel fuel and gasoline in the Ontario
market under the Cleaner Transportation Fuels (CTF) regulation. At least 75 percent of the renewable
content required in diesel fuel (and 64 percent of the renewable content required in gasoline) must be
produced in Canada. This announcement made Ontario the second province in Canada to announce
domestic content requirements.

The measure is expected to be time-limited and temporary in nature, and the ministry will monitor the
situation impacting the domestic biofuels industry.

The minimum 75 percent Canadian content requirement was prorated to 25 percent for the remainder of
the 2025 compliance period due to the shorter timeframe for compliance. For subsequent compliance
years the minimum domestic content requirement will remain at 75 percent on an annual basis.

Canola meal, domestic consumption

In MY 2026/27, canola meal use in Canada is forecasted to increase from the previous marketing year,
on the assumption of a larger dairy herd size, and increased supplies. Canola meal is increasingly
favored in Canadian dairy rations due to its ability to boost milk production and its competitive cost
compared to soybean meal.

In MY 2025/26, canola meal consumption is forecasted to increase marginally on larger dairy herds.

Canola meal is used domestically as livestock feed, primarily for dairy cattle, but also for swine, poultry,
and fish. Canola meal is also used in pet foods.

An increase in domestic crush capacity has prompted interest in ways to utilize canola differently,
diversify exports into markets where livestock production is growing (e.g., IndoChina), and increase
meal’s nutritional and economic value. Recently, a chemical engineering team from Queen’s University
was granted funding to conduct a research project exploring uses of canola meal as a material feedstock.



Canola meal, trade

Canola meal exports are forecast to increase by more than five percent in MY 2026/27 over the previous
year, and by less than one percent in MY 2025/26. Demand in MY 2025/26 is strong in its primary
market of the United States and also in secondary markets such as in the EU; however, exports are
constrained by supply.

Canada’s canola meal exports are generally tied to domestic supply and have hovered around 88 percent
of domestic production for the past ten years. Post’s MY 2025/26 and MY 2026/27 canola meal export
forecasts assume that this production-trade relationship will continue.

The United States remains the largest buyer of Canadian canola meal. Canola meal export prices to the
United States remain stable relative to seed and oil. But in MY 2025/26, especially in October and
February, the monthly average unit prices of canola reached the lowest monthly average since at least
2009, primarily due to Chinese retaliatory tariffs of 100 percent on imports, restricting access to its
second-largest market.

Canada is not a significant importer of canola meal, due to its oversupply of domestic production.

Soybean production – MY 2025/2026

In MY 2025/2026, national soybean production decreased nearly 11 percent over the previous marketing

Soybean trade– MY 2026/2027

Imports of soybeans will remain limited by soybean processing capacity and sufficient domestic oilseed
supplies (i.e. canola). Most soybean commodity volume is imported in meal form for animal feed.

Soybean imports are forecasted to increase 13 percent on increased demand from domestic processors
due to increased demand from Canadian renewable fuel producers in eastern Canada, and the reduction
in domestic production in 2025.

Canada is also a global leader in producing quality, sustainable, food-grade soybeans for international
markets. However, starting in 2025, Canadian farmers faced new administrative burdens: only soybeans
proven to be produced on land that has not been deforested since 2020 are eligible for export to the
European Union (EU). Although the implementation date is in 2025, this restriction also impacts crops
harvested in 2024. The EU has been one of Canada’s largest and most stable export markets for
soybeans. Over the preceding years, it has represented 23 percent of Canadian soybean exports.

Soybean meal production – MY 2026/2027

In the absence of soybean meal extraction rates for MY 2026/27, a five-year average (MY 2021/22 to
MY 2025/26) was used to determine the rate. Similar to oil, the meal extraction rate does not change
significantly from year to year, ranging from 0.716 to 0.78 MT of meal derived from a metric ton of
soybeans over the five-year period.



Soybean meal production – MY 2025/2026

With Statistics Canada no longer publishing data on meal, since July 2025, FAS/Ottawa used the five-
year average (MY 2020/21 to MY 2024/25) meal extraction rate, and the estimated crush level, to
estimate MY 2025/2026 production.

FAS/Ottawa’s MY 2024/25 meal production figure is also an estimate. Statistics Canada began
suppressing soybean data in July 2025, which means the final month of the marketing year is an
estimate.

While extraction rates for MY 2025/26 are not published, soybean meal extraction in Canada generally
averages about 0.76 percent per year. The extraction rate represents the weight of soybean meal
produced relative to the total weight of the processed soybeans. Approximately 75 percent of a
soybean's weight is converted into soybean meal, while 20 percent is oil and five percent is hull.



Soybean meal exports – MY 2026/2027

Canada is a minor player compared to other major soybean meal suppliers like Brazil and Argentina.
Soybean meal production is expected to make up only 15 percent of Canada’s total meal production (i.e.
soybean meal and canola meal) in MY 2026/27, and only about 29 percent of soybean meal production
will be exported.

An increase in exports to the United Kingdom in MY 2025/26 could be an indication of an upward trend
that will continue into MY 2026/27, driven by United Kingdom retailers’ voluntary commitments to
deforestation-free soy in food and feed. FAS in the United Kingdom reports that these commitments are
expected to boost demand for North American origin soybean meal over the next two years.



Soybean meal exports – MY 2025/2026

To date (August to February), exports are up 23 percent over the same period last year. This is primarily
due to an increase in exports to the United Kingdom and the United States. They report, “While the
UK’s Forest Risk Commodities legislation is still pending, demand has surged for North American
soybean meal in UK animal feed rations as a proxy for a verifiably low deforestation-risk supply.”

Sunflower seed production, MY 2026/2027

Sunflower seed production is forecast to decrease 13 percent in MY 2026/27 over the previous year,
based area planted data provided by Statistics Canada’s planting intentions survey, and the assumption
that yields will fall in line with the five-year average (2021/22 to 2025/26) of 2.23 MT/ hectare.

Sunflower seed is not a major oilseed in Canada and imports remain limited due to limited processing
capacity.



Sunflower seed production, MY 2025/2026

Statistics Canada reports that 69,000 MT of sunflower seeds were produced in 2025, a 35 percent
increase over the previous year, driven by a 29 percent increase in area planted, and improved yields
over the previous year. The previous year saw the smallest area planted to sunflower seeds since MY
2011/12, due to large stocks and unfavorable market prices.

Manitoba produced 96 percent of Canada’s sunflower seed in 2025 (the highest share since 2017),
owing to the fact that 97 percent of area planted to sunflower seed was in Manitoba. Industry contacts
estimate that in MY 2025/26 Manitoba sunflower seed area consisted of 4,717 hectares of confection
and 21,405 hectares of oilseed. In the year prior, Manitoba area consisted of 4,993 hectares of confection
and 7,963 hectares of oilseeds. Area planted to sunflower oilseeds grew as MY 2024/25 stocks were
worked down and market prices improved. Post is not aware of breakdowns in other provinces.
