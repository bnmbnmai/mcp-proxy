   INCIDENT SUMMARY:

   At 1138 hours on 24 May 2026, a fire occurred at Main Pass (MP) 298 A located on Lease
   G01315. MP 298 A is a production platform owned and operated by Cantium, LLC
   (Cantium). The fire was observed in the vicinity of the Gas Compressor and Glycol
   Dehydration System. The fire was extinguished by the production operator with a water
   hose. The platform was shut in and remained out of service pending investigation and
   verification of the equipment’s condition prior to restart. There were no personnel
   injuries or pollution as a result of this incident.


   SEQUENCE OF EVENTS:

   On 24 May 2026, at approximately 1138 hours, a small flash fire was observed by the
   Cantium Production Operator during his morning rounds. The Operator was responding to
   a Pressure Alarm Low (PAL) for the glycol pump when the operator observed a fire on
   the top deck. The fire was observed near the Glycol Dehydration System and where the
   Gas Compressor (located on the lower deck) exhaust piping penetrates through the top
   deck. The Operator immediately grabbed a nearby water hose and quickly extinguished
   the flame on the top deck. As he continued his inspection of the area, he noticed
   another flame on the Gas Compressor exhaust piping, on the lower deck. The Operator
   immediately went down to the lower level and used another water hose to extinguish the
   flame on the lower deck. Once both fires were extinguished, the Operator shut down
   both the Compressor and Glycol Dehydration system. The Operator then notified Cantium
   management of the event and then continued to monitor and cool down the area with
   water to prevent re-ignition.

   Shortly after being notified of the incident, Cantium personnel arrived on location
   and initiated evidence preservation and began an incident investigation. Cantium’s
   investigation consisted of collecting witness statements, equipment photographs,
   safety system information, operational timelines, and affected equipment
   documentation.


   BSEE INVESTIGATION:

   On 29 May 2026, the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE)
   investigation team arrived at MP 298 to perform their investigation. The team
   consisted of a New Orleans District (NOD) Accident Investigator (AI), a NOD Lead
   Inspector (Lead) and an additional Investigator with the Office of Incident
   Investigations (OII). The team requested and reviewed documentation of the event,
   photos of the incident location, interviewed personnel on the facility, and performed
   a walkthrough of the incident area as well as the rest of the facility.

   Cantium’s investigation determined that glycol leaked from a failed hose connection on
   one of the glycol pumps (PBA 1210/11). The glycol leaked onto solid decking, flowed
   across the deck approximately 8 feet until it reached a breach in containment caused
   by corrosion (pinholes). The pinholes in the deck allowed the glycol to drip onto the
   gas compressor’s hot exhaust piping at the deck penetration. When the glycol contacted
   the compressor’s hot exhaust piping, the glycol ignited.

   As a result of reviewing the documentation provided to the investigation team, the
   BSEE investigation aligned with Cantium’s Root Cause Analysis (RCA), which suggested
   that a loose glycol pump hose fitting allowed glycol to leak onto the solid deck below
   the pump. The glycol then “flowed” across the solid deck until it leaked through
   pinholes in the containment deck and then impacted the compressor hot exhaust piping

   During the investigation, the Platform Operator stated that the glycol pump had been
   replaced a week earlier. As such, it’s possible that the hose fittings may not have
   been properly secured/ tightened after the pump was replaced. As a result of the
   incident, Cantium personnel did a “blitz” inspection of their facilities to look for
   similar containment deficiencies across their entire fleet. There is a Hot Work Plan
   in place to correct all these containment breeches while the platform is shut in.


   IN CONCLUSION:

   After performing a walkthrough of the facility, and investigating the incident
   location, the BSEE investigation team determined that this incident was caused by poor
   containment which allowed glycol to drip through pinholes in the containment deck onto
   a hot compressor exhaust on the deck below. Once the glycol came into contact with the
   hot exhaust piping, the fire occurred.

   BSEE recognizes that leaks from production equipment are common throughout the Gulf of
   America and are experienced by all companies involved in the production of oil and
   gas. The primary method of minimizing the effects of leaks is through good containment
   systems beneath the equipment.

   The investigation revealed that Cantium had been operating the facility with multiple
   breaches in containment. As a result of this fire, Cantium has allocated additional
   resources to address similar containment issues across this facility and other Cantium
   facilities across the gulf.




   18. LIST THE PROBABLE CAUSE(S) OF ACCIDENT:

   Human Performance Error: Inattention to task.

   • Personnel failed to adequately secure or tighten the fittings associated with the
   glycol pump installation after swapping out the glycol pump.

   • Personnel failed to identify containment breeches in the Glycol Dehydration System
   skid.

   19. LIST THE CONTRIBUTING CAUSE(S) OF ACCIDENT:
   Equipment Failure: Inadequate equipment maintenance or equipment repairs.

   • Containment breeches under the Glycol Dehydration System allowed glycol to leak onto
   the hot exhaust piping of the Gas Compressor where it ignited.
